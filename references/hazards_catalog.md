# Hazards Catalog

## welding-fumes

- Harm: Inhalation of metal fumes and hazardous gases.
- Typical injuries: Respiratory irritation, metal fume illness, and possible chronic health effects depending on the substance and exposure.
- Work types: welding.
- PPE: respirator.
- Rules: respiratory-protection.
- Controls: Local exhaust ventilation, exposure assessment, and suitable work practices.
- Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.252

## flying-particles

- Harm: Particles may strike or enter the eyes and face.
- Typical injuries: Eye irritation, corneal injury, cuts, and facial injuries.
- Work types: grinding.
- PPE: safety-glasses.
- Rules: eye-face-protection.
- Controls: Machine guards, suitable screens, and safe operating practices.
- Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.133

## fall-from-height

- Harm: Falling to a lower level or through an opening.
- Typical injuries: Fractures, head injuries, spinal injuries, and fatal injuries.
- Work types: roof-work, excavation.
- PPE: safety-harness.
- Rules: fall-protection.
- Controls: Guardrails, covers, suitable work platforms, and personal fall protection where required.
- Sources:
  - https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.501
  - https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.140

## high-noise

- Harm: Excessive sound exposure can damage hearing.
- Typical injuries: Noise-induced hearing loss and tinnitus.
- Work types: grinding.
- PPE: hearing-protection.
- Rules: hearing-conservation.
- Controls: Quieter equipment, noise reduction, exposure monitoring, and hearing conservation.
- Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.95

## chemical-exposure

- Harm: Chemicals may enter the body through inhalation, skin or eye contact, or ingestion.
- Typical injuries: Irritation, dermatitis, chemical burns, poisoning, or other substance-specific effects.
- Work types: chemical-handling.
- PPE: respirator, chemical-gloves, safety-glasses, safety-boots.
- Rules: respiratory-protection, eye-face-protection.
- Controls: Substitution, closed transfer systems, ventilation, labels, and safety data sheets.
- Sources:
  - https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200
  - https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.134
  - https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.138

## trench-collapse

- Harm: Soil or excavation walls may collapse and trap or crush workers.
- Typical injuries: Crushing injuries, suffocation, fractures, and fatal injuries.
- Work types: excavation.
- PPE: hard-hat, safety-boots.
- Rules: excavation-safety, head-protection.
- Controls: Properly designed protective systems, competent-person inspections, and keeping loads away from excavation edges.
- Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.652

## arc-radiation

- Harm: Welding arcs emit intense visible, ultraviolet, and infrared radiation.
- Typical injuries: Arc eye, eye inflammation, and skin burns.
- Work types: welding.
- PPE: welding-helmet.
- Rules: eye-face-protection.
- Controls: Welding screens, restricted access, and suitable shielding.
- Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.252

## burns

- Harm: Contact with hot surfaces, sparks, molten material, or corrosive chemicals.
- Typical injuries: Thermal burns, chemical burns, and tissue damage.
- Work types: welding, grinding, chemical-handling.
- PPE: welding-helmet, safety-boots, chemical-gloves.
- Rules: eye-face-protection.
- Controls: Heat shielding, safe handling procedures, chemical substitution, and separation from hot or corrosive materials.
- Sources:
  - https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.252
  - https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.138

## PPE selection note

PPE listed here is a simplified learning mapping. It does not establish that an item is sufficient for every exposure. Select PPE according to the hazard assessment, material or chemical properties, equipment instructions, and applicable standards.
